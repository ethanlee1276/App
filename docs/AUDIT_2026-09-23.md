# Product audit — 2026-09-23

Ethan's "$100K Product Audit" of the public site, saved in full at the
bottom of this file so it can be checked off. The tracker above it
gives each numbered item a verdict, what the site already does, the
plan, and a status. Update the status line in the same commit that
does the work.

**Status words:** DONE (with the commit) · IN PROGRESS · NOT STARTED ·
NEEDS ETHAN (a decision or a fact only Ethan can supply) · NOT DOING
(with the reason).

**Ground rules taken from the audit itself (item 22):** no new
features while this list is open. Every change makes something that
exists clearer, faster, easier to trust or easier to buy. Nothing on
the public record is hidden or regraded; how it is presented can
change.

## Tracker

### 1. Too much, too early — collapse the navigation
- **Verdict:** partly agree.
- **Already there:** the desktop sidebar shows six pages and folds the
  rest under four headings (Betting, Library, My Book, Proof). The
  audit's tool read the folded items as well, so the list it quotes is
  longer than what a visitor sees at first.
- **Plan:** regroup the folds to the audit's names — Picks, Odds,
  Research, My Book, Proof — in the sidebar and the phone's More sheet;
  move Predict, Fantasy and Radar out of the top of the sidebar.
  Keep the phone tab bar as Home · Picks · Live · Results · More: the
  Live tab is what a bettor opens during games, so it stays, and Odds
  and Research sit one tap down in More.
- **Status:** IN PROGRESS.

### 2. The public "live" experience looks stale (Aug 17 slate, "Sample data")
- **Verdict:** agree — the first fix.
- **Already there:** the badge reads "Sample data" only when the served
  board was not built from live data (`generated_from`), and a stale
  bar appears when the build is days old.
- **What this machine cannot see:** the droplet. An Aug 17 slate means
  a board published that day was still being served. Check on the box:
  `cd /srv/qellys && python3 homecheck.py` (board dates per sport), or
  read `generated_from`, `date` and `built_at` in
  `web/data/recommendations.json`.
- **Done (code side, whatever the cause):** a demo board carries a
  banner across the top of the page — "Demo board. These are example
  games and prices … not today's slate, and nothing here is a pick" —
  with a link to the real record, and the corner badge reads "Demo
  data". A real board whose newest game is a week or more behind
  today says "This board's games are from … N days ago", even when
  the build that carried it is fresh.
  `tests/test_a_demo_or_old_board_never_passes_for_todays.py`.
- **Status:** DONE in code; the droplet check above is still Ethan's
  to run, to find out why an old board was being served.

### 3. Brand leak: "Zeno's Picks"
- **Verdict:** Ethan's call. Zeno is intentional — the page describes

- **Recommendation:** keep the name and say who Zeno is wherever the

- **Status:** NEEDS ETHAN.

### 4. The killer feature is buried — lead with verifiability

### 9. Stop making users interpret the model — tier labels
- **Verdict:** agree for probability, disagree for edge.
- **Why:** probability tiers (Lean · Play · Strong · Premium) fit the

  coin flip, and the site says so. Calling a +12% edge "Significant"
  is the overclaim the site exists to refuse.
- **Plan:** probability bands only, taken from the same calibration
  bands the Record shows.
- **Status:** NOT STARTED.

## The audit, as Ethan sent it (2026-09-23)

Qellys Book — $100K Product Audit

I reviewed the public-facing Qellys Book site, the pricing/checkout messaging, Terms, Privacy Policy, and compared the product positioning against several established betting-analytics products currently in the market. I did not pretend to audit screens behind the authenticated paywall because this environment cannot securely log into the demo account.

The blunt assessment

Qellys Book has a better underlying product concept than the current website communicates.

The problem isn’t a lack of features. It is almost the opposite: you’ve built too much product into the first impression.

Right now, Qellys Book feels like a powerful betting terminal that happens to have a subscription page.

It needs to feel like a premium product that happens to contain a powerful betting terminal.

That’s the biggest difference between where it is and where I’d want a $100K build to land.

⸻

1. The biggest problem: too much, too early

Your navigation currently exposes an enormous amount of functionality:

Dashboard, Top Picks, Long Shots, Live Now, My Bets, Record, Value Bets, Game Lines, Line Shopping, Futures, Injuries, Weather, Trending, Rosters, Players, Rankings, Alerts, Streak, Bankroll, The Lab, Zeno’s Picks, Methodology, Status, Why Us, Features, Prediction Markets, Market Scanner, Most Likely to Hit, Rocket Radar, UFC, Fantasy, and more.

That’s impressive from a product-development perspective.

It’s not ideal from a UX perspective.

A new user shouldn’t have to understand 25 different concepts before understanding what to do tonight.

I’d reduce the primary navigation to roughly:

HOME

PICKS

ODDS

RESEARCH

MY BOOK

Everything else becomes nested.

That one change alone would make the product feel considerably more expensive.

2. Critical issue: the public “live” experience looks stale

The public page currently labels itself “Sample data”, but the displayed slate is dated August 17, 2026, including the record and last slate. The site also describes itself elsewhere as providing “real-time” sports intelligence.

Today is September 22, 2026.

That’s a 36-day discrepancy.

If this is intentionally demo/sample data, I would make that visually unmistakable:

DEMO MODE

Example data shown for demonstration. Sign in to view today’s board.

If it is supposed to be real public data, this is the first thing I’d fix.

A betting analytics product cannot look stale.

Even if the underlying data is excellent, a visitor seeing an August 17 slate in September can immediately think:

“This thing isn’t active.”

That is a conversion killer.

⸻

3. There is still a brand leak: “Zeno’s Picks”

This is one of the easiest things to fix and one of the things I’d fix immediately.

The current site still contains “Zeno’s Picks” in the product architecture.

That’s a leftover brand.

Your customer should never have to wonder:

“Wait, what is Zeno?”

Unless Zeno is intentionally a permanent internal character/product, rename it.

Much cleaner.

⸻

4. Your actual killer feature is buried

The site’s strongest differentiator isn’t “we have NFL picks.”

There are hundreds of products that say that.

Your strongest proposition is:

The model makes a quantified claim, records the exact price, and publicly grades itself afterward.

That’s much more interesting.

The homepage already explains that every pick is recorded with its price, date and result, and that the Record remains free.

I’d make this the central brand concept.

Something like:

DON’T TAKE OUR WORD FOR IT.

Every pick is timestamped.

Every price is recorded.

Every result is graded.

Every loss stays on the board.

VIEW THE RECORD →

That is a real brand position.

Most pick-selling websites try to convince people they are winners.

Qellys can position itself around verifiability.

⸻

13. I’d seriously consider a longer trial

Your competitors demonstrate that 7-day trials are common in this category.

Props.Cash currently advertises a 7-day free trial, and OddsJam’s current offerings also advertise 7-day trials.

Three days is enough for someone who already understands Qellys.

It’s short for a new user.

The problem is that sports betting products have different value depending on the schedule.

Someone might sign up Sunday afternoon and not fully understand the product until Monday.

I’d test:

Option A

7-day free trial

versus

Option B

3-day free trial + $1 first month

versus

Option C

Free account + limited daily picks + paid upgrade

Don’t pick based on opinion.

A/B test it.

14. The biggest missing business feature: analytics

Your Privacy Policy explicitly says Qellys currently runs no analytics, advertising, tracking pixels or third-party scripts.

That’s privacy-friendly.

But for a company trying to grow, you need to know:

Where did the subscriber come from?

Which page converted them?

What did they view before subscribing?

What features correlate with retention?

Where did the trial user stop?

What percentage of users reach a pick?

How many people use line shopping?

How many open the Record?

You don’t need to build an ad-tracking monster.

Build privacy-conscious first-party product analytics.

Track events such as:

view_top_pick

open_pick

view_record

open_line_history

use_line_shop

start_trial

complete_checkout

cancel_trial

return_day_2

return_day_7

subscription_renewed

That would fundamentally improve decision-making.
