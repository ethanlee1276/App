# The site audit — 30 October 2026

You asked, 2026-10-30: *"look at all the features we have and see what we can
build off of, see what we can add more of, see what there needs to be done to
make all these features feel complete and the website feel complete ... a
full, long, complete audit ... make sure we have everything that needs to be
added and everything that is added is complete and done and ready for the
public use."*

This is that audit. It is written for reading on a phone: what was checked,
what was wrong and is now fixed, what only you can do, the state of every
page, and what to build next, in order. The commands for the box are step IK
of `docs/WHEN_YOU_ARE_HOME.md` (`catchup.sh IK` runs every read-only one).

## How it was checked

- **Every page, as a free account, at a phone's width and a desktop's**, in a
  real browser, on a copy of the site serving the live public data from
  qellysbook.com (the files a free account actually reads), and every page
  again in the member's view. Screenshots, page errors, console errors,
  sideways scrolling and empty pages recorded for each — see *Page by page*.
- **Every public data file on the live site**: whether it exists, how big it
  is and when it was last written.
- **The live box's own health file** (heartbeat.json): every board's build,
  its counts, its self-check, the cycle time, the disk.
- **The code behind each feature**, read for unfinished paths, stale copy,
  features that never got switched on, and gaps the calendar will open.
- **Every claim the site makes about itself** (the plans page, Features,
  the glossary, the locked cards, the description search engines read)
  against what the site actually does.

What could **not** be checked from here: a member's boards with real picks
(the paid copies live only on the box), real payments, emails and phone
pushes. Those are covered by the test suite (about 16,900 tests, 960 of them
in a browser, green on every push) and by your own daily use.

## Fixed in this round

1. **The Pick of the Day is free** (your ask). Every league's card on one page;
   a free account's Home opens it instead of the plans. The board's best bet
   on a day the pick sits out, the lean it turns down and the pick tracked
   live stay members-only.
2. **Today's Top Pick never came from the NFL.** The NFL board is dated by
   week ("2026-W08"), so its card never read as today's and was skipped on
   every NFL pick day since the top pick shipped on 15 September.
3. **Today's Top Pick never looked at hockey or the fight card**, which have
   named a pick of the day since October.
4. **The Discord feed could post a declined lean as the Pick of the Day.** It
   read a field the engine never wrote. It now posts only a pick it would bet
   (and the pick, being free, on every channel).
5. **The news section has never shown a headline.** The feed read sent the
   site's own User-Agent, which ESPN answers 403; the file on the box was
   written once, empty, on 1 September. Fixed, with CBS Sports beside ESPN
   for every league, hockey added, and the reason any feed fails written into
   the file.
6. **The book report card on Line Shopping has never ranked a book.** It read
   a price field the line history has never written ("odds"; the history
   writes "over_odds" and "under_odds", one row per book), so every snapshot
   read as no price, nothing was measured and the file was never published.
   Its tests passed on a row shape of their own. It reads the real rows now:
   one snapshot's books together, one line and one game at a time, both
   sides, nothing after the start (an in-play price is not a close), over the
   last four weeks, with only the games not yet played held in memory. The
   tests now write their rows with the history's own writer. The card fills
   after the next daily chores.
7. **The NFL board's self-check flagged a posted pick whose note and tile
   disagreed** (heartbeat: `LOCK NOTE 1`). A posted pick that drops under the
   bar says "Our chance at this number is now 52%"; the books' price could
   then lower the chance again, and the note kept the number from before. The
   note now follows the tile, and the two round the same number.
8. **The Discord page promised a first post that would never come** — no
   webhook is set, so nothing ever posts. It now says the site's own posts are
   not switched on yet.
9. **The plans page never mentioned the NHL** ("Every sport: NFL, MLB, NBA,
   WNBA, CFB and UFC"), and now says the Pick of the Day is free.
10. **The Live tab's locked card said members see the Pick of the Day.** It
    now says what is locked — the picks tracked live — and that the pick
    itself is free.
11. **The glossary defined the Pick of the Day wrongly** (as one pick across
    every league; it is one per league).
12. **The Features page had stopped a month ago.** Pick'em and its groups,
    karma and levels, the closing-line leaderboards, Call it, trivia, today's
    question, the player-of-the-game vote, watch parties, Remind me, the Edge
    Report, Your data and the weekly limit were never written up. All there
    now — and the list moved out of the first visit (−5 KB for every visitor).
13. **The NBA Cup page named group leaders before a game was played** (at
    0-0, on our ratings alone).
14. **There was no robots.txt or sitemap.xml** — both paths returned the
    page's HTML. Both exist now; crawlers are kept off the data files (one is
    13 MB) and the API.
15. **The site's description for search engines left out the NHL.**
16. **The Status page said "running starting (0 min)"** for a chore that had
    just started. It says "starting".

   fix what they show.
3. **Check that the news feeds answer the box** — the read-only check is in
   step IJ. If ESPN and CBS both refuse the box, the file now says why.
4. **The Terms' list of free features is out of date** (it lists My Bets,
   alerts, the community and the games; the free tier now also has every live
   game, the Record, Fantasy and the Pick of the Day). Nothing in it is wrong,
   so no notice is owed — but say the word and I will add one sentence.
5. **Decide the free tier's edge.** Standings, rosters, injuries, weather and
   the races are facts, not picks, and the paywall already publishes their
   facts free — but the pages are locked for a free account. Opening them
   would make the free side feel fuller at no cost to the paid side. Your

6. **Switches waiting on you since earlier rounds:** the yearly upgrade offer

## What to build next

Nothing new for its own sake: each of these finishes something the site
already does, or carries it into the part of the calendar it is about to
reach. In order.

1. **The NBA Cup's knockout rounds** (needed by early December). The Cup page
   follows the groups only; the quarterfinals, the semifinals and the final in
   Las Vegas are not followed. The page should become the bracket once the
   groups finish: the eight teams, each game with our line, results as they
   land.
2. **The College Football Playoff after selection day (6 December).** The race
   page stops at the selection. It should turn into the bracket: the twelve
   seeds, the first round on campus, the quarterfinals and semifinals at the
   bowls and the final, each game with our line and the result.
3. **An NFL playoff race.** The race page covers college and the Cup; the NFL
   has none, and from mid-November its playoff picture is the most-read thing
   in the sport. The same engine (play out the rest of the season with our
   game model) gives each team's chance at the playoffs, the seeds if it
   ended today, and the Sunday games that swing it.
4. **Make the free pick travel** (each your call):
   - A free alert for the Pick of the Day: one a day, opt-in, "today's pick is
     up" — never "bet now". Free accounts can have alerts for their teams but
     not for the pick.
   - Today's pick on the sign-up screen and the plans page: a graded, public
     pick is the best argument the site has, and it is free now.
   - A share card for the pick, like the karma card: the pick, the price, and
     the result once it settles.
5. **Open the free side** if you decide to (needs you 5): one switch per page.
6. **Read the book report card once it fills** (after tomorrow's chores). If
   it says a book we treat as sharp prices worse than the median book, that
   is a finding for the pricing side; nothing changes until you have seen it.
7. **Split the college rosters file** (13.3 MB, every roster in one file;
   only the rosters page and search read it). Per-team files would let the
   page read a few kilobytes. Low priority — it is compressed on the wire and
   nothing else loads it.
8. **RS6, BALLDONTLIE** — waiting on a key.
