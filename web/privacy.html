Privacy Policy

Last updated 29 October 2026

1. The short version

  • We collect your email address, a hash of your password, and whatever you choose to put into the Service (a bet log, fantasy settings).
  • We run no advertising, no tracking pixels and no third-party scripts. We count how the site is used — visits, which pages and games are opened, questions asked in Ask, bets put in My Bets, alerts turned on and sent, limits and breaks set, plan buttons pressed, and trials and subscriptions started or ended — as daily totals only. The totals carry no cookie, no identifier and no IP address, and cannot say what any one person did. Nothing on this site follows you anywhere else.
  • We never see your card details. Stripe takes the payment on its own page; we hold a customer reference and a status.
  • We do not sell or share your personal information, and we never have. There is no advertising business here to sell it to.
  • You can delete your account and everything attached to it from the account panel, yourself, at any time.

2. What we collect, and exactly where it lives

This is the complete list. Each item names the table it is stored in, so it can be checked against the code.

2.1 Account

  • Email address — your login, and how we reach you about your subscription. (users.email)
  • Or your mobile number, if you made the account with your phone instead — then it is your login, a code we text you is how you sign in, and the account has no email address and no password (its email column holds a placeholder nothing can be sent to). We use the number to sign you in and for nothing else. (users.phone)
  • A password verifier — a scrypt hash. We do not store your password and cannot recover it; we can only reset it. (users.verifier)
  • Passkeys, if you add one — Face ID, Touch ID or a phone’s own lock in place of a password. For each: the id your device gave it, its public key (the half that can only check a signature), the count the device keeps of its uses, a name for it, and when it was added and last used; and one random handle the passkeys carry for your account in place of its number. Your face, your fingerprint and the passkey’s private key never leave your device. Each sign-in’s one-time challenge is kept for three minutes at most. (passkeys, passkey_users, passkey_challenges)
  • Account created and last seen timestamps (last seen is refreshed at most once a day while you are signed in). (users.created_at, users.last_seen)
  • That you confirmed you are 21 or over, and when you accepted the Terms. (users.age_confirmed, users.terms_accepted_at)
  • A record of each yes and no you give us about your data or our messages — the 21+ and Terms confirmation, alerts turned on (which kinds) or off, a break from alerts or a limit you set — with the date of this policy at the time, and when. A change of mind is a new line, never an edit, so the record says what you chose and what you were shown. It is in Download my data and deleted with your account. (consent_log)
  • Session tokens, stored hashed, with an expiry. The cookie in your browser is the only copy of the raw token; a stolen database does not yield a working session. (sessions.token_hash)
  • Password reset links, stored hashed, if you use “Forgot password?”. The link in your email is the only working copy; we keep its one-way scramble for an hour, or until it is used, and delete it then. (password_resets.token_hash)

2.2 What you put in

  • Your bet log — the wagers you choose to record: sport, market, price, stake, result. Entered by you, for your own record-keeping. We do not place these bets and cannot see any account you placed them on. The Edge Report on My Bets — your return by market, how the prices you took did against the close, your best- and worst-priced bets, the hours you usually bet — is worked out from this log and the closing prices in §2.7, for you alone. (user_data)
  • Your parlays’ night, for the replay — while the games of a parlay you saved to My Bets are on, the chance we give it each time it is graded: before the start, through the games (a point every two minutes, or sooner when it moves two points), and how it ended, so My Bets can play the night back. Kept 30 days. (slip_chance)
  • Your settings — bankroll size, unit size, fantasy league preferences, alert choices. (user_data, sections bankroll and fantasy)
  • Your search history on this site — the player and team searches you run here, kept so the search box can offer them back to you. This is our own search box only; it has nothing to do with anything you search anywhere else, and there is a button on the account page to clear it. (user_data, section search)
  • Your preferences — how prices and stakes are shown, your time zone (your alerts’ quiet hours follow it), the leagues in your menu, the teams and players you follow, whether the bar wears your team’s colours, what you told us you are here for and the books you bet at (the “Make it yours” card, or Settings; your books lead the Bet it sheet), and a weekly limit, if you set one (Settings → Limits). (user_data, section settings)

Those five sections — mybets, fantasy, bankroll, search, settings — are the complete list of what the synced part of an account holds. It is the same list the code enforces (accounts.SECTIONS). The features in §2.7–2.9 keep their own rows, each named there.

2.3 Subscription

  • A Stripe customer id and subscription id, the status, the paid-through date, which plan, and the date the subscription began. That is the entire payment-related record on our side. The date it began is Stripe's own, and tells us when a monthly plan has reached its third bill, when we may offer the yearly plan once. During a free trial, also the trial end we last sent the one reminder email about, so it goes once; and, on a six-month or yearly plan, the renewal we last told you about, so that notice goes once too. (subscriptions)

  • A month you give. If you buy a month for a friend, the gift code, which account bought it and when, the Stripe Checkout it was paid in, and, once it is used, which account used it and when — so a code works once and you can see whether yours has been. Deleting either account takes it off the gift; the gift stays bought, and stays used. (gift_codes)
  • Discount codes you redeemed, and failed attempts, the latter only to rate-limit guessing. (code_redemptions, code_attempts)

2.4 Server logs

Our web server keeps ordinary access logs — IP address, timestamp, path requested, user agent — as every web server does. They are used for security and debugging, are not joined to your account, and are not used to build a profile of you. Your IP address is also read at request time to enforce security rules (for example, refusing a password over an unencrypted connection); it is not written to any database table.

A security log records sign-ins, failed passwords, refused codes and blocked requests, so that an attack on an account can be seen: the time, what happened, the network the request came from (the first three parts of an IPv4 address, or the first three groups of an IPv6 one — not your full address) and, for account events, a short one-way tag made from the account’s email. It never holds a password, a code, a session token or the email itself, it is not joined to your account, and it is rotated as it grows.

2.5 Usage counts

  • Usage counts (analytics.counts): per day, an event name, the name of the page (or, for a few events, one detail of it: a game’s league, how a bet was put in My Bets, what the “Make it yours” card was answered with), a broad source (direct, search, social, another site, a campaign link, or one of our alerts) and whether the visitor was signed in or subscribed, with a count; and how many alerts reached phones, by kind (picks up, a final score and so on), as a count. No identifier of any kind.

2.6 Crash reports

When the site hits a script error on your screen, it sends us the error’s message, where in our code it happened, the page you were on, your screen’s size and your browser’s family (for example “Safari iOS”). That is all: no IP address, no account, no identifier, nothing you typed. We read them to find and fix bugs; the file is rotated as it grows.

3. What we do NOT collect

  • Your sportsbook password, or any betting-account credential. We never ask for them and there is nowhere on this Service to enter one. A DraftKings or FanDuel login can move real money and cannot be scoped or revoked by us, so we do not want it.
  • Card numbers, expiry dates or security codes. These are typed on Stripe’s page and never reach our servers.
  • Advertising or tracking cookies. There are none. No third-party analytics tags, no pixels, no fingerprinting, no third-party scripts of any kind.
  • Your location. We do not request it, infer it, or store it.
  • Your contacts, photos, files or device identifiers.
  • Anything about children. See §9.

5. Cookies

We set one cookie: a session cookie that keeps you signed in. It is HttpOnly (JavaScript cannot read it), SameSite-restricted, and marked Secure when served over HTTPS. It contains a random token and nothing about you.

Some preferences (theme, bankroll display), the profiles you opened from Social’s Search (its Recent list: a username, display name and colour, eight at most), a pick’em group’s invite you opened before you could join it (until you join) and how many times this phone has asked you to turn alerts on, and when it last did (so it asks three times at most, two weeks apart) are kept in your browser’s local storage. That never leaves your device and we cannot read it.

There are no advertising, analytics or third-party cookies, which is why this site has no cookie banner: there is nothing to consent to.

  • Backblaze, Inc. — encrypted backups of the database are copied to their B2 storage service so that a failed disk is not the end of your account. The backup contains the same data described in §2 and nothing more. It is stored in a private bucket that only we can read.

We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, as those terms are used in US state privacy law. We have never done either.

We may disclose information if legally required to — a valid subpoena, court order or comparable process. Where we are permitted to tell you, we will.

7. How long we keep it

  • Account data — until you delete your account.
  • Your bet log and settings — until you delete them, or your account.
  • Subscription records — deleted with your account, except where a payment record must be retained for tax or accounting purposes. Stripe keeps its own transaction records under its own retention rules.
  • Sessions — expire on their own, and are deleted when you sign out or change your password.
  • Password reset links — one hour, or until used, whichever comes first; deleted when you change your password.
  • Failed code attempts — rolled off after one hour.
  • Processed webhook ids — 60 days.
  • The closing prices of your saved parlays’ legs — 90 days; their chance through the night, for the replay — 30 days. The record of your yeses and noes — until you delete your account.
  • Feed posts — 120 days, or until you delete them or your account. A game’s chat — 14 days. Direct messages — the latest 500 between two people, until either account is deleted.
  • Server logs — a short rolling window for security and debugging.

Backups outlive deletion, and we would rather say so. The account database is included in a weekly encrypted backup — it has to be, or one failed disk erases the records of every user who did not ask to be deleted. We keep six weeks of those archives. So an account you delete disappears from the live site immediately and can still exist in an archive for up to six weeks, after which it is gone from those too. Backups are used only to restore the service after a failure; they are never queried, analysed or shared.

8. Your rights, and how to use them

Wherever you live, you can:

  • See what we hold — Settings → Your data → What we keep about you lists every kind of record we hold about you, how many and how long each is kept, with every yes and no you gave us; the records themselves are in Download my data;
  • Correct it — change your email or password from the account page;
  • Delete it — the account panel deletes your account and everything attached to it. You do not have to ask us, and we do not put a retention flow in front of it;
  • Export it — Download my data on the account page gives you everything we hold, as JSON, immediately and without asking us;
  • Clear your search history — Clear search history on the account page, separately from the rest;
  • Object or complain — email us.

The first three are buttons, not requests. Delete my account on the account page removes your account and every section attached to it. We do not put a retention offer, a phone call or a waiting period in front of any of them.

Deleting your account, step by step (the same in the app and on the website): sign in, open Account from the menu, choose Delete my account and confirm with your password (a live subscription is cancelled first, from the same page, so you are never billed for an account that is gone).
